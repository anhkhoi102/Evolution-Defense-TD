`EvolutionDefense/Art/` draws it at launch. The asset manifest

## 2. Target art direction

**"Stylized 2.5D low-poly diorama":** chunky, readable shapes, soft sun
lighting with real cast shadows, a slightly desaturated natural palette, and
saturated team and accent colours for gameplay elements.
* **Bad North:** perspective, lighting and depth.
* **Kingdom Rush:** tower readability and upgrade escalation.
* **Iron Marines:** silhouettes and combat effects.

Readability rules:
* **Gameplay layer:** towers, enemies and projectiles are saturated, outlined by rim light, and always above decoration.
* **Decoration:** trees and rocks are lower contrast and never overlap the path or build pads.
* **Per-era identity:**
  * Primitive: wood, bone, stone.
  * Medieval: stone, iron, banners.
  * Industrial: brass, steel, smoke.
  * Futuristic: white alloy, cyan energy.

  All four eras share the same palette rules and lighting.
* **Map tint:** the existing per-era tint stays as a subtle grade on top.

## 3. The core sourcing problem

Evolution Defense spans **four eras, from wild animals and stone towers to
combat robots and orbital weapons**. Every 2D tower-defense pack found covers
**one** setting, almost always fantasy-medieval. Covering the other three eras
with 2D packs means mixing artists and styles, which the brief rules out.

The option that stays consistent across all eras is the **CC0 low-poly 3D
libraries** by Kenney and Quaternius. Each library is internally consistent,
and both use a very similar flat-shaded style. They cover:
* Nature and terrain.
* Medieval castles and siege weapons.
* Tower-defense parts.
* Animated animals, monsters, robots and mechs.
* Sci-fi structures and weapons.

**Rendered offline** into sprite sheets with one camera and one light rig,
they become 2.5D sprites with real shadows and smooth animation. That is
essentially how Bad North looks. SpriteKit only ever sees PNG atlases, so no 3D
framework is added to the game.

## 4. Options compared

| | **A. CC0 3D kits → baked 2.5D sprites (recommended)** | B. 2D hand-painted TD packs (CraftPix and similar) | C. 2D pixel-art TD packs (Foozle Spire etc.) | D. Commission original art |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Look | Bad North-like low-poly diorama, real lighting and shadows | closest to Kingdom Rush (painted vector) | retro pixel art | anything |
| Covers all 4 eras in one style | **yes** (same authors across fantasy, nature, monsters, robots, sci-fi) | **no**: fantasy only; industrial and sci-fi would come from other artists | no (fantasy) | yes |
| Perspective control | **any angle**, re-renderable at will | fixed by the artist (mostly side or 3/4 view) | top-down | as ordered |
| Animation | rigged models with walk, death, attack and more; any frame count | a few frames per unit, varies by pack | 4–8 frames | as ordered |
| Tower upgrade variants | built from modular parts: 5 visibly distinct levels per tower | some packs have 3 levels | 3 levels | as ordered |
| Licence | **CC0** (Kenney). CC0 on Quaternius pack pages, but see §7 | CraftPix licence: commercial use OK, no redistribution of source files, no AI/ML use; premium needs a subscription | mostly free; terms vary per pack | full ownership |
| Retina quality | rendered at any resolution (@2x by default) | vector sources are good; PNGs vary | pixel art only scales by integers | as ordered |
| Cost | free (optional donations) | free tier is limited; premium is a subscription | free | high |
| Main risk | needs Blender installed; primitive-era towers and a few creatures need kit-bashing | style mismatch across eras | doesn't match the requested style | budget and time |

**Recommendation: Option A.** It is the only option that meets *consistency*
and *full coverage* together without commissioning art. The pipeline also keeps
the asset-replacement architecture intact: a later commissioned pack just
replaces the manifest.

## 6. What changes technically (gameplay untouched)

All of this stays within the existing asset system (manifest → ArtLibrary →
renderers). No gameplay value changes.

1. **Offline bake pipeline** (`Tools/bake/`, a Blender Python script plus a JSON scene list):
   * One orthographic camera at the chosen elevation (see §9) and one key-light and fill rig.
   * Shadow-catcher ground and an outline pass, rendered at @2x.
   * **Enemies:** 8 directions (5 rendered, 3 mirrored), each walk animation sampled at 8–12 frames and death at 6–8.
   * **Towers:** bases are static. Heads are rendered at **16 aim angles**, with 3–4 firing frames each.
   * The bake trims, packs and writes `.spriteatlas` folders plus manifest entries. Re-running it regenerates everything.
2. **Renderer additions**, driven by the manifest:
   * **Facing mode `directional`:** picks the frame by heading instead of rotating the sprite, which is correct in 2.5D.
   * **World→screen projection:** applied in the renderers only, a vertical squash for the 3/4 view. Range circles become ellipses.
   * **Depth sorting by ground y:** enemies, towers and decorations interleave correctly, replacing today's fixed layers.
   * **SKEmitterNode effects:** pooled and capped in performance mode, for fire, smoke, frost, sparks and the EMP ring.
   * **SKShader:** hit flash, burn, freeze tint, stealth shimmer, and a dissolve on death.
3. **Map:** rebuilt as a baked diorama.
   * Terrain tiles, the path as a dirt road with edges, and decoration placed by the existing seeded scatter rules, which keep it clear of the path and pads.
   * Water and cliffs only outside the playfield.
   * The gameplay path and build-slot coordinates stay exactly as they are.
4. **Atlases & memory:**
   * One atlas per era for enemies, loaded when the era starts and freed when it ends.
   * One shared atlas for towers, one for effects and one for UI.
   * Estimated resident texture memory: **~120 MB peak** (all four eras would be ~250 MB), comfortable on an 8 GB M1.
5. **UI:** Kenney UI skin (9-slice panels and buttons) plus a display font with a free licence (OFL), applied through `Theme`. SwiftUI layout is unchanged.
6. **Audio:** the AudioManager gains a file source (`AVAudioFile`) per effect id, next to the synthesizer. The synthesizer stays as the fallback.

## 7. Licensing plan

* **Licence inventory.** `Docs/ASSET_LICENSES.md` will list every integrated
  file: pack, author, source URL, version and download date, licence (with the
  licence text saved in `ThirdParty/Licenses/`) and what it is used for. It is
  added with the first imported asset.
* **Kenney:** CC0, confirmed on the official pages. Attribution isn't required;
  I'll credit Kenney anyway on a credits screen.
* **Quaternius:** pack pages say **CC0**. The site also publishes a "Quaternius
  Asset License v1.0", which allows commercial games with no attribution but
  forbids redistributing the raw assets. Inside a compiled game that is fine,
  but source models must not be committed to a public repository. I'll record
  the licence of the exact files downloaded. Raw `.blend`/`.fbx` files stay
  out of git: they go in `Tools/bake/sources/`, ignored by git, with a download
  script. Only the rendered atlases ship.
* No asset extracted from a commercial game is used. The reference games
  inform direction only.

## 8. Known gaps (custom work inside the same style)

The kits don't contain everything ready-made. These will be **kit-bashed** in
Blender from the same CC0 parts, so the style stays consistent:

* **Primitive towers** (Stone Thrower, Scout Archer, Spear Thrower, Sling
  Tower): wooden platforms and stone rings from the Nature and Castle kits,
  plus Mini Characters crews.
* **Primitive base:** a palisade village from Nature Kit logs and tents.
* **Industrial towers:** steel platforms from the Tower Defense Kit plus
  cannon and turret parts, given a brass and steel material swap.
* **Orbital Strike Platform, Plasma Artillery:** Space Kit dishes and
  turrets, with emissive materials.
* **Possible creature gaps**, to confirm on download: boar, troll, dragon,
  mutant titan. The fallbacks, in order: closest monster recoloured and
  rescaled; another CC0 Quaternius pack (Bestiary, Animated Monster);
  scaled-up parts of an existing model.
* **Projectiles and effects** are simple shapes. They will be modelled or
  painted to match, with particle textures from the Particle Pack.

## 9. Decisions needed before Stage 2

1. **Source strategy:** approve **Option A** (CC0 3D kits baked to 2.5D sprites).
2. **Perspective:**
   * **Recommended: 3/4 "Kingdom Rush" view** (camera about 50° above the ground, looking straight up the map, not rotated). The current rectangular map, path and pads keep their layout and screen shape.
   * **True 45° isometric** (Bad North) puts the 2:1 rectangular map on a diamond. It leaves large empty corners in a 16:9 window and shrinks the playfield by about 30%, unless the map is re-authored, which touches gameplay data.
3. **Tooling:** allow installing **Blender** for the offline bake (`brew install --cask blender`, the official build, about 1 GB on disk). It is a development tool only; the game never links it. Without it, Option A isn't practical.
4. **Audio:** replace the synthesized sound effects with Kenney's recorded CC0 packs (recommended), and keep or replace the music.
